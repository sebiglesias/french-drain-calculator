How French Drains Are Engineered for Decades of Flow

A true French drain is a subsurface trench filled with washed aggregate surrounding a perforated pipe, completely wrapped in permeable filter fabric (a "burrito wrap"). It works on the principle that water follows the path of least resistance through gravel pores 1,000 times faster than through compacted clay soil.

1. Always Point Perforations DOWN: One of the most common DIY mistakes is installing the pipe with holes facing up. When holes point down at 4 o'clock and 8 o'clock, rising groundwater enters the bottom of the pipe and is carried away immediately before it can saturate your lawn. Holes facing up allow silt to settle into the pipe and require the entire trench to flood before drainage begins.

2. Use Washed #57 Stone (3/4" to 1"): Never use gravel with "fines" or crusher run. Fines (limestone dust or clay) pack tight, trap silt, and turn your gravel bed into concrete within two rain seasons. Always specify double-washed round river rock or #57 crushed granite.

3. Never Use Woven Weed Barrier: Woven landscape weed barrier fabric has microscopic slit openings that quickly coat with clay particles, creating a waterproof barrier that blinds the drain. Always use 4 oz or 6 oz needle-punched non-woven geotextile, which maintains water flow for 30+ years.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the minimum slope required for a French drain?
The minimum slope is a 1% grade, which equals a 1-inch vertical drop for every 10 linear feet of trench (or 1/8 inch per foot). This ensures stormwater moves quickly enough to scour out any minor sediment without stagnation.
Where should a French drain discharge?
French drains should discharge to daylight in a lower portion of the property using a pop-up emitter valve, into a dry well, into a municipal storm sewer (where permitted by local code), or into a rain garden at least 10 to 15 feet away from any structure foundation.
